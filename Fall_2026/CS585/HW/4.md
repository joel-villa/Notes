## Question 1

Adaptability is an important factor in designing a CDN server selection strategy. A server can become over-burdened resulting in reduced performance, a mobile end-device can physically move further from a CDN server. For these reasons, and many more, a CDN server selection strategy must be pessimistic and adaptable. This can be as simple as routinely probing the Local DNSs from various CDN clusters in a process known as real-time measurement, or something more complex. 
Scalability is another important factor in design a CDN server selection strategy. A CDN server can become easily overwhelmed if more and more LDNSs are making requests of it. This is one of the reasons why a geographically closest strategy, though a simple to implement heuristic, will not always be best.

## Question 4

Of the four organizations identified in the previous question, the FCC seems the most likely to intervene in such a circumstance. For one, the victims are within the United States, which falls within the FCC's jurisdiction. Secondly, the FCC has actual enforcement power, i.e. if the phishing website is doing something that the FCC has deemed illegal or fraudulent, than the FCC would definitely have the ability to act to enforce their regulations. 
Two of the four organizations could not meaningfully act in such a scenario, the IETF and RIPE NCC. RIPE NCC, is based in Europe, and their primary output is a domain name database, which makes it too many degrees of separation from such a circumstance. The IETF makes standards, so potentially if such a phishing scheme could be easily solved by bolstering Internet standards, than maybe they could act, but this too feels like a reach in terms of meaningful action.
