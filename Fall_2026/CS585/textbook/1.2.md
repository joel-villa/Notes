    2. Massively parallel computing infrastructure to do data processing

## 1.2.1 Access Networks

- <u>Access Network</u>: "the network that physically connects an end system 
  to the first router (also known as the “edge router”) on a path from the end 
  system to any other distant end system"

### Home Access: DSL, Cable, FTTH, and 5G Fixed Wireless

* QUESTIONS:
    * If DSL is reliant on preexisting telephone infrastructure, and cable 
      reliant on preexisting TV infrastructure, is there a more optimal 
      infrastrucutre which could be made to distribute Internet access? this 
      question feels poinant as the Internet is usurping the roles that both
      cable and telephone used to play (Fiber To The Home may be this)
    * Why is the downstream allocated higher transmission rate? Likely due to 
      more Recieve actions than Send actions when interfacing with the 
      Internet
- Two most common broadband residential access: DSL and cable
- Digital Subscriber Line (DSL): Makes use of existing local telephone 
  infrastructure
    - Typically provided by telephone company (telco)
    - Customer's telco is also their ISP
    - The home's DSL modem converts digital data to high frequency tones to 
      transmit over telephone wire
    - Residential telephone lines carry both data and telephone signals, 
      encoding at different frequencies
    - Downstream rates: 24 Mbs and 52 Mbs
    - Upstream rates: 3.5 Mbps and 16 Mbps
        - Access is asymmetric (upstream rate $\ne$ downstream rate)
    - DSL is meant for short distances (if residence is not within 10 miles of 
      the Central Office, they are fucked)
    - Digital Subscriber Line Access Multiplexor (DSLAM):
        - Converts analog signals back to digital format inside the Central 
          Office
        - RECALL: Multiplexor takes many inputs and gives a singular output
- Cable Internet Access: Makes use of existing cable TV infrastructure
    - cable head end: The cable company's control facility
    - coaxial cable: copper wire
    - <u>Hybrid Fiber Coax</u> (HFC): both fiber and coaxial cable used to 
      distribute 
      Internet
    - Cable modem termination system (CMTS): turns analog signal sent from 
      home modems back into digital format (akin to DSLAM)
- Cable modems divide the network into both an upstream and downstream channel
    - Asymmetric, with downstream allocated higher transmission rate
- Cable Internet is a shared broadcast medium:
    > "In particular, every packet sent by the head end travels downstream on 
    > every link to every home and every packet sent by a home travels on the 
    > upstream channel to the head end. For this reason, if several users are 
    > simultaneously down- loading a video file on the downstream channel, the 
    > actual rate at which each user receives its video file will be 
    > significantly lower than the aggregate cable down-stream rate. On the 
    > other hand, if there are only a few active users and they are all Web 
    > surfing, then each of the users may actually receive Web pages at the 
    > full cable downstream rate, because the users will rarely request a Web 
    > page at exactly the same time. Because the upstream channel is also 
    > shared, a distributed multiple access protocol is needed to coordinate 
    > transmissions and avoid collisions."
-  <u>Fiber To The Home</u>(FTTH): Direct Fiber
    - 'One' fiber per home: fiber leaves the central office and splits when it 
      gets relatively close to the homes
    - Two competing optical-distribution network architectures which  do the 
      splitting:
        1. Active Optical Networks (AON) - switched Ethernet
        2. Passive Optical Networks (PON)
- Passive Optical Network (PON):
    - One Optical Network Terminator per home (ONT)
    - ONT connects to an optical splitter
    - optical splitter combines a number of cables into a single, shared 
      optical fiber which connects to the OLT
    - Optical Line Terminator (OLT) lives in the telephone company's center 
      office, converts between optical and electrical signals
